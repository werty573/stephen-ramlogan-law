Criminal Law

Representation for people who are being questioned, charged or prosecuted for a criminal offence.

Being questioned or charged by the police is frightening, and what happens in the first hours can matter a great deal. You have the right to speak with an attorney, and you should use it.

The firm advises clients from the earliest stage, applies for bail, and represents clients through summary matters in the Magistrates' Court and indictable matters in the High Court.

Matters the firm handles

  • Advice during police questioning
  • Bail applications
  • Summary offences in the Magistrates' Court
  • Indictable matters in the High Court
  • Traffic and motor vehicle offences
  • Pleas and mitigation
  • Appeals, where available
  • Advice for witnesses and victims

How a matter usually proceeds

  1. First contact

    Call the office as early as possible. If someone has been detained, tell us where and when.

  2. Advice and bail

    We advise on rights and next steps and, where appropriate, apply for bail.

  3. Preparing the case

    We review the evidence disclosed by the prosecution and prepare the defence with you.

  4. In court

    We represent you at each hearing and explain every stage before it happens.

If you or a family member has been arrested, you have the right to communicate with an attorney. Call the office during hours on 868-322-5551.

What to bring to your first meeting

  • Any charge sheet, summons or bail document
  • The name of the police station and officers involved
  • Contact details for people who saw what happened
  • Any documents or messages connected to the matter

Common questions

Should I speak to the police without an attorney?

You have the right to consult an attorney, and it is wise to do so before answering questions about an allegation. The firm can advise you on what to expect.

What happens at a bail hearing?

The court decides whether a person can be released while the case continues, and on what conditions. An attorney can put forward reasons and conditions that support release.

Can a charge be dealt with without a trial?

Sometimes. Depending on the evidence and the charge, there may be options such as a plea, and an attorney can advise on whether any of them is in your interest.
