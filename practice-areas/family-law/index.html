Family Law

Clear, discreet advice on separation, divorce and arrangements for children.

Family matters are personal, and they are often urgent. Whether you are thinking about separation, need arrangements for your children, or are dealing with maintenance, you need advice that is clear about the law and careful with your circumstances.

The firm advises and represents clients in divorce, custody, access and maintenance matters, and in dividing property after a marriage or relationship ends. Where agreement is possible, it is usually the better route, and we will help you reach it.

Matters the firm handles

  • Divorce and separation
  • Custody and access to children
  • Child and spousal maintenance
  • Division of matrimonial property
  • Variation of existing orders
  • Agreements between parties
  • Cohabitational relationships
  • Protection orders

How a matter usually proceeds

  1. Private consultation

    A confidential meeting to understand your family, your concerns and what you want for the future.

  2. Options

    We explain the routes available, from agreement and mediation to applications before the court.

  3. Documents

    We prepare the petition, application or agreement and handle service on the other party.

  4. Resolution

    We represent you at hearings and work towards orders that are fair and workable.

If you or your children are in immediate danger, call the police on 999. A protection order can be applied for urgently.

What to bring to your first meeting

  • Marriage certificate and children's birth certificates
  • Any existing court orders or agreements
  • Details of income, property and regular expenses
  • Any letters or documents from the other party

Common questions

Do we both need lawyers for a divorce?

Each party should have independent advice. The firm can act for one party only, and will deal with the other party or their attorney directly.

How is custody decided?

The court's main concern is the welfare of the child. Arrangements agreed between parents are often approved where they serve the child's interests.

Will my matter be kept private?

Yes. What you tell the firm is confidential, and family proceedings are not open to the public in the way other court matters are.
