Wills & Estates

Planning ahead with a properly made will, and guiding families through an estate after a death.

A properly made will lets you decide who receives your property and who will deal with your estate. Without one, the law decides, and families are often left with delay, expense and disagreement.

The firm prepares wills and powers of attorney, and assists executors and families in obtaining a grant of probate or letters of administration, collecting assets and distributing the estate.

Matters the firm handles

  • Preparing and updating wills
  • Powers of attorney
  • Grants of probate
  • Letters of administration
  • Estates where there is no will
  • Transfer of property to beneficiaries
  • Advice for executors
  • Disputes over estates

How a matter usually proceeds

  1. Conversation

    We discuss your family, your property and how you would like it to pass.

  2. Draft

    We prepare a draft will or application and go through it with you line by line.

  3. Signing

    The will is signed and witnessed correctly, which is essential for it to be valid.

  4. Safekeeping

    We advise on keeping the original safe so it can be found when it is needed.

A will that is not signed and witnessed correctly may not be valid. It is worth having it prepared by an attorney.

What to bring to your first meeting

  • Identification
  • A list of property, accounts and policies
  • Names and details of the people you want to benefit
  • For an estate: the death certificate and any will found

Common questions

What happens if someone dies without a will?

The estate is distributed according to rules set by law, and someone usually has to apply to the High Court for letters of administration before property can be dealt with.

How often should I update my will?

Review it after major life events such as marriage, divorce, the birth of a child or the purchase or sale of property.

What is a power of attorney?

A document that allows someone you trust to act for you, for example to sign documents while you are abroad. Its terms should be drafted to suit exactly what you need.
