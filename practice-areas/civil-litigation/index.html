Civil Litigation

Advice and representation when a dispute with a person or a business needs to be settled or taken to court.

Most civil disputes start long before anyone files a claim. A contract is not honoured, a debt goes unpaid, a neighbour causes damage or an accident leaves someone injured. Getting advice early can make the difference between a matter that settles and one that goes to trial.

The firm advises on where you stand, writes to the other side, negotiates where that serves you and, where it does not, prepares and conducts the claim through the courts.

Matters the firm handles

  • Breach of contract
  • Recovery of money owed
  • Personal injury claims
  • Motor vehicle accident claims
  • Negligence and other torts
  • Landlord and tenant disputes
  • Property damage
  • Defending claims made against you

How a matter usually proceeds

  1. Review

    We look at your documents, the timeline and what you want to achieve, and tell you plainly where you stand.

  2. Pre-action letter

    In many cases a formal letter to the other side sets out the claim and invites a response before court.

  3. Negotiation

    Where a fair settlement is possible, we negotiate it and put it in writing.

  4. Proceedings

    If needed, the claim is filed and managed through case management, disclosure and, if it comes to that, trial.

Strict time limits apply to civil claims in Trinidad and Tobago, and they differ depending on the type of claim. If you think you have a claim, do not wait to get advice.

What to bring to your first meeting

  • Any contract, receipt, invoice or agreement
  • Letters, emails and messages with the other side
  • Photographs, police reports or medical reports, where relevant
  • A short timeline of what happened, with dates

Common questions

Do I have to go to court to settle a dispute?

Not always. Many disputes settle after a formal letter or through negotiation. Court is one option, and the firm will tell you honestly whether it is the right one for your matter.

Someone has filed a claim against me. What should I do?

Contact a lawyer promptly. Court documents come with deadlines for responding, and missing them can lead to judgment being entered against you.

How long does a civil claim take?

It depends on the complexity of the matter, whether the other side defends it and the court calendar. You will be given a realistic view at the start and kept informed as it moves.
