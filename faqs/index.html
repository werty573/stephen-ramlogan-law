Frequently asked questions

Short answers to the questions clients ask most often. For advice on your own matter, request a consultation.

Do I need an appointment?

Yes. Consultations are by appointment so that time is set aside for your matter. Call the office on 868-322-5551 or send a consultation request.

What are the office hours?

Monday to Saturday, 8:00 am to 4:00 pm. The office is closed on Sundays and public holidays.

Is what I tell the firm confidential?

Yes. Communications with your attorney for the purpose of legal advice are confidential and protected by legal professional privilege.

Does contacting the firm make it my attorney?

No. The firm acts for you once it has confirmed there is no conflict and you have agreed the scope of the work. Until then, please share only a brief description.

How much will my matter cost?

It depends on the type and complexity of the matter. Many document and property matters can be quoted as a fixed fee, while court matters are usually charged in stages. You will be told before work begins.

Are stamp duty and registration fees included?

No. These are government charges, separate from legal fees. They are shown separately so you can see exactly what is paid to the State.

What if I cannot afford an attorney?

You may be eligible for assistance from the Legal Aid and Advisory Authority of Trinidad and Tobago, which helps eligible people in certain criminal, family and civil matters.

Will I have to go to court?

Not always. Many civil and family matters are resolved by agreement. Where court is needed, you will be told what to expect before every hearing.

I have been served with court papers. What now?

Contact an attorney promptly. Court documents carry deadlines, and missing them can lead to orders being made without you being heard.

A family member has been arrested. How can the firm help?

Call the office as early as possible with the person's name and the police station. A detained person has the right to communicate with an attorney.

What is the difference between RPA title and common law title?

Land under the Real Property Act has a certificate of title issued by the State. Common law title is proved through a chain of deeds recorded at the Registrar General's Department.

Do I need a lawyer to buy a house?

It is strongly advisable. An attorney checks the title, prepares the deed and attends to stamping and registration so the property is properly transferred to you.

What happens if someone dies without a will?

The estate passes according to rules set by law. Usually someone must apply to the High Court for letters of administration before property can be dealt with.

What is the difference between probate and letters of administration?

Probate is granted to the executor named in a will. Letters of administration are granted when there is no will, or no executor able to act.
